An estate planning attorney can learn what matters to you, explain legal options, and help prepare documents such as wills or trusts. The right scope depends on your situation, so a first conversation is a chance to identify questions rather than assume every client needs the same documents.

Steps, questions, and useful terms

A planning discussion often begins with your goals and the information needed to understand your circumstances. The attorney can then discuss which legal tools may fit and what work is proposed. You can help make that conversation productive by noting your questions and gathering relevant information about your wishes and planning concerns.

Consider asking:
- What documents or legal advice are part of the proposed work?
- Are probate, trust, elder law, or tax issues relevant to my questions?
- What information should I provide, and what timing should I expect?
- How is the work billed, and what might change its scope?

You may hear these terms:
- Will: A legal document that records instructions for after death.
- Trust: A legal arrangement for holding or managing assets under stated terms.
- Probate: A court process that may apply to handling an estate after death.

Ask the attorney to explain how any term relates to your own circumstances. The labels alone don’t tell you which planning choices are suitable.
