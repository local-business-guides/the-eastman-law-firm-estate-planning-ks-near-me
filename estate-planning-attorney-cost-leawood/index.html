What the work may include

Estate planning is more than choosing a document name. An attorney can discuss your goals, explain relevant options, and prepare wills or trusts as appropriate. Depending on your circumstances, you may also want to ask whether probate, trust administration, elder law, or tax questions belong in the conversation.

When comparing providers, ask each one to describe the work in similar terms. Find out what documents and advice are included, whether follow-up or revisions are part of the proposed scope, and what could lead to additional work. Avoid comparing a narrow document preparation task with a broader planning service as if they were the same.

A few terms you may hear:
- Will: A legal document that sets out instructions for after your death.
- Trust: A legal arrangement that can hold and manage assets under stated terms.
- Probate: A court process that may be involved in handling a person’s estate after death.

The details and effect of these tools depend on the circumstances, so ask the attorney to explain how each applies to your plan.
